# NeetCode Review: What the Free Tier Really Covers, What Pro Adds at $119/Year, and When a Pattern-First Course Fits Better

Most people searching for a NeetCode review aren't trying to find out whether NeetCode is any good. They already watched a handful of the videos, liked the teaching style, and now they're staring at a pricing page asking a narrower question: is $119 a year — or $297 once — worth paying when the videos I just watched were free?

That's a fair question, and it has a real answer. It also has a second half that most reviews skip: what NeetCode doesn't do, and whether that gap is the thing actually slowing you down.

## What NeetCode actually is

NeetCode began as a YouTube channel and grew into a prep site built around curated problem roadmaps — most famously the NeetCode 150, plus Blind 75 and an extended list. The core pitch is sequencing: two pointers before sliding window, trees before graphs, in an order that builds pattern recognition instead of throwing 4,000 problems at you.

The free tier isn't a teaser. You get the roadmaps and the video explanations for the curated lists without paying anything. That's the single most important fact about NeetCode, and any review that buries it is wasting your time.

One wrinkle worth knowing before you budget: NeetCode's own pages disagree with each other. The Pro page describes 200+ videos and 300+ curated problems. The pricing page has been quoted showing "1,000+ practice problems." Those are almost certainly counting different things — the umbrella library versus the curated Pro set — but nobody at the company has spelled that out publicly, so treat the bigger number as unverified rather than as a bonus you're buying.

## What NeetCode Pro adds, item by item

Pro is best understood as convenience and tooling bolted onto content that mostly exists already. As listed on the Pro page:

- **200+ videos** and 300+ curated problems with an in-browser editor, test cases, and progress tracking
- **Written guides** for people who'd rather read than watch
- **Solutions in eight languages** — Python, Java, C++, JavaScript, C#, Go, Swift, Kotlin
- **NeetBot**, an in-editor assistant that answers questions and generates visual walkthroughs
- **AI hints and debug**, including progressive hints and a side-by-side diff of your code against a suggested fix
- **Company-tagged problems across 100+ companies**, filterable by interview frequency
- **Cloud sync** of your code and **a private Discord**
- **Courses**: a 2026 catalog listing 11 courses and roughly 144 hours, including two 10-hour system design courses, two 8-hour object-oriented design courses, and Python and SQL tracks

The genuinely useful upgrade is integration. Instead of running a roadmap in one tab, YouTube in a second, LeetCode in a third, and your notes somewhere else, you study, code, and ask for help in one window. If your prep keeps collapsing because you can't stay consistent, that friction removal is worth more than any individual feature.

If you already know you want the tooling and not just the roadmap, you can 👉 [compare NeetCode Pro's plans directly](https://neetcode.io/pro) — noting that NeetCode runs its own promotions, so the figure at checkout is the one that counts.

### The pricing, and the arithmetic people get wrong

As listed on the Pro page, the two options are:

- **One year: $119**, marked down from $199, with auto-renew off by default
- **Lifetime: $297**, marked down from $599, one-time

That's a 2.5-year break-even. Two years at $119 costs $238, which is cheaper than lifetime. Three years costs $357, which isn't. So lifetime only math out if you expect to be back for a third hiring cycle — plausible if you job-hop every couple of years, less plausible if this is your first serious prep push.

Two caveats on those numbers. First, a lot of review posts still quote $149/year and $219 lifetime. Those figures were accurate at some point; they aren't on the current Pro page. Second, the auto-renew-off default is the most underrated line in the whole checkout flow, because it means the yearly plan doesn't quietly renew while you're busy being employed.

## Where NeetCode stops being enough

None of this is a knock on the product. It's where the product ends.

**You still need LeetCode for volume.** Around 300 curated problems builds recognition. It doesn't build the speed that comes from having solved several hundred, and speed is what gets tested in a 45-minute screen.

**There's no interview simulation.** You can solve problems; you can't rehearse the interview. No time pressure, no one watching, no follow-up questions about how your solution breaks at 100× load.

**System design is introductory.** Two 10-hour courses are a real addition, but if you're interviewing at L5 and above, that round needs 20–40 hours of dedicated work on distributed systems, consistency models, and scaling trade-offs. NeetCode gives you a starting point, not a preparation.

**Behavioral prep is basically absent.** For Amazon in particular, where the behavioral round carries its own weight, that's a separate purchase somewhere else.

**Video is a passive format for the skill that matters most.** This is the subtlest limitation. Watching someone arrive at the right approach teaches you the reasoning but doesn't train the identification step — the two minutes at the start of a problem where you decide which pattern applies. Progress tracking on any platform records that you visited a section, not that you internalized it.

> Takeaway: NeetCode Pro is worth $119 if your bottleneck is *organization and consistency*. It's not the right purchase if your bottleneck is *spotting the pattern fast under pressure*.

## The case for a text-first, pattern-first course

That identification gap is exactly what AlgoMonster was built around. The platform is built by a team of ex-Google engineers, and its structure is deliberately un-NeetCode-like:

- **48 explicitly named patterns**, each with a conceptual explanation, a code template, and problems mapped to it
- **231 lessons and problems**, plus 500+ lessons across 6 courses, 1,000+ illustrations, and 3,000+ editorials on the current plan page
- **Decision flowcharts** for problem categorisation — sorted input? contiguous subarray? tree or graph? — that walk you to the likely pattern before you write code
- **Speedrun mode** for timed review
- **An AI assistant** with unlimited usage on paid plans
- **Company question sets** updated monthly

The format is text and diagrams, no video at all. That's a deliberate trade, not an oversight, and it cuts both ways: reading is faster to skim and search than re-watching a 10-minute walkthrough, but if you've built your whole study habit around NeetCode's videos, switching formats mid-prep is a real cost.

The curriculum is also finite, which most prep platforms aren't. It's designed to be worked through in roughly 4–8 weeks at an hour or two a day. If you have an interview in six weeks, a course with an endpoint beats a problem bank with 4,029 entries and no finish line.

## How to decide

**Take NeetCode free if** you're within your first hundred problems or two. The roadmaps and videos cover pattern recognition at that stage, and the friction of switching tabs is a smaller problem than deciding what to study.

**Take NeetCode Pro if** you're already working through NeetCode material and the thing breaking your consistency is scattered tools. The editor, progress tracking, company tags, and AI hints exist to remove that scattering. $119 for a year, and the auto-renew default means you won't be surprised later.

**Take AlgoMonster if** you write code fluently but lose time at the start of problems, you'd rather read a template than watch a walkthrough, and you have a 4–8 week window. At the current listed pricing, the lifetime tier is cheap enough that the payback question mostly disappears — which is the opposite of how NeetCode's $297 lifetime works out.

**Take both if** you have the budget and a specific deadline. The pairing that actually makes sense is AlgoMonster for the pattern framework in the first weeks, then LeetCode for volume so the patterns survive contact with unfamiliar constraints. NeetCode Pro on top of that is redundant unless you specifically want the company-tagged lists.

And if you're two weeks out from an interview, buy nothing. Neither course produces retention that fast.

## FAQ

**Is NeetCode Pro worth $119 a year?**
It depends on whether you're paying for content or for tooling. The videos and roadmaps are free; Pro adds the editor, progress tracking, company tags, AI hints, courses, and Discord. If you're disciplined enough to maintain your own tracking, the free tier carries a lot of the load.

**Does NeetCode Pro have company-tagged problems?**
Yes — the Pro page lists company tags covering 100+ companies, sortable by frequency. That used to be LeetCode Premium's exclusive pitch, and it isn't any more.

**Is AlgoMonster a replacement for NeetCode?**
Not really — they attack different problems. NeetCode curates existing LeetCode problems around a roadmap; AlgoMonster teaches pattern templates and maps its own problems to them. If videos are how you learn, NeetCode. If you'd rather read, skim, and search, AlgoMonster.

**Do either of them cover system design and behavioral rounds properly?**
No. Both have some system design content, neither goes deep enough for senior-level design rounds, and neither offers meaningful behavioral preparation. Budget a separate resource for those.
